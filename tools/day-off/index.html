    How it works

    Three things worth knowing

    01 The law, not a calendar trick

    In the first year it is one day per month of full attendance, up to 11. From the one-year anniversary it becomes 15 days, and from the third year one more day for every two years beyond the first, up to 25. This tool counts from your hire date, not from January.

    02 Unused leave has a deadline

    Leave generally lapses one year after it accrues, so the last day to use it is the day before your next anniversary — or 31 December under a calendar-year scheme. If the leave could not be used because of the employer, it carries over or is paid out instead.

    03 Holidays are the official list

    The built-in data follows the Regulation on Public Holidays of Public Offices as amended on 30 April 2026 — including Labour Day (1 May) and Constitution Day (17 July), new that year — and the substitute-holiday rule that moves a weekend holiday to the next free weekday. Temporary holidays are not included.

    Questions

    Straight answers
    Will this match the number my company gives me?
    It matches the statutory default. Companies may use a calendar-year basis, prorate a partial first year, round fractions differently, or grant more in their rules of employment. Use this as the floor the law guarantees, then check your contract — where the two differ, the company's obligation is the better of the two.
    Why only 11 days in the first year?
    Article 60(2) gives one paid day for each month of full attendance in the first year, which reaches 11 before the anniversary. On the anniversary the 15-day entitlement of Article 60(1) starts fresh, so the two are never added together.
    How are substitute holidays treated?
    When a holiday overlaps a Saturday, a Sunday or another holiday, it moves to the next non-holiday. New Year's Day and Memorial Day never move. The planner counts those moved days as free time, which is why some windows reach nine days without a single leave day in the middle.
    Does my hire date leave the browser?
    No. The calculation runs in this tab, nothing is stored on a server, and the counters in the header and footer read the page's own network activity. There is no endpoint to send a date to.
